# Readouts

A readout is a display showing a value: 7-, 8-, 9-, 14-, 16- and 22-segment displays, Nixie tubes, the 5 × 7 matrix, and notations like Cistercian and Maya numerals, Braille, Morse and semaphore. `TYPES` lists them all. Many readouts share one canvas and one sign. This submersible's console is one overlay, each instrument a readout following its bezel:

A `createPanel` draws on a canvas you place, each readout at `at` and `height` in its CSS pixels. A `createOverlay` is one canvas fixed over the viewport, each of its readouts fitted inside the element it `follow`s and clipped where that element's scrolling ancestors clip it:

```ts
const overlay = createOverlay({ gpu: handle });
const score = overlay.readout({ type: 'nixie', cells: 4, follow: card.querySelector('.score') });
score.set(1984);
```

A `:` or `.` after a digit lights that cell's colon or point. `face: 'none'` drops the dark faceplate, leaving only lit segments over the page. A readout also takes `color`, `slant`, `align`, a leading ½ digit (`half`) and `defects` by cell and segment.

## Transitions

`transition` sets how `set` moves a readout to its next value, and `transitionMs` how long that takes. Every name in `TRANSITIONS` is shown here. A type that can't spell a step, such as a bargraph under `flap`, fades instead, and so does `count` for anything that isn't a number. Typing into a readout never animates.

`createTransition` makes one from its settings: what each cell does (`cut`, `fade`, `step` through a string of characters, `scramble` or `count`), which cells go first (`left`, `right`, `center`, `edges` or `random`), how far apart their turns start (`spread`, 0 to 1), and an `easing`. Every built-in is made this way and carries its `recipe`, so `createTransition({ ...TRANSITIONS.flap.recipe, through: ' 0123456789' })` is a flap of digits. For anything else, a `Transition` of your own is given the old and new cells and returns how brightly each light shows at each moment.

## Selecting and typing

A readout whose type spells letters or numbers can be selected and copied like text: dragging across its cells selects the characters they show, and double- and triple-click take a word or the whole readout. `selection` picks how a selected cell shows. `edit: true` makes one you can type into, with `onInput` hearing each change, and `lines` stacks rows of cells. `select: false` turns this off, which an overlay readout over something clickable wants, since its cells take the pointer.
